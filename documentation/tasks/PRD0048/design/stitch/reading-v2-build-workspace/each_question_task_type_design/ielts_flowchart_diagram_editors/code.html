The bicycle is a remarkable piece of engineering that has undergone numerous transformations since its inception. While early models were rudimentary and often difficult to ride, advancements in materials and manufacturing processes eventually led to the efficient machines we use today. One crucial development was the process of vulcanization.

Vulcanization, a chemical process that converts natural rubber into a more durable material, revolutionized the bicycle industry. Before this, tires were made of iron or wood, providing a harsh and uncomfortable ride. The introduction of the pneumatic tire, made possible by vulcanization, significantly improved comfort and speed.

Another iconic design from the 19th century was the Penny Farthing. Characterized by its massive front wheel and tiny rear wheel, it allowed for higher speeds but was notoriously unstable and dangerous. The rider sat high above the ground, making falls a frequent and hazardous occurrence.
