Passage Text

The bicycle is a remarkable piece of engineering that has evolved significantly over the past two centuries. Initially conceptualized as a simple wooden frame with two wheels, it has transformed into a highly efficient mode of transport and recreation. The earliest iteration, often attributed to Karl Drais in 1817, was known as the "running machine" or Laufmaschine. It lacked pedals, requiring the rider to push off the ground with their feet to generate forward momentum.

It wasn't until the 1860s that pedals were attached directly to the front wheel, giving birth to the "velocipede," popularly known as the "boneshaker" due to its rigid frame and iron-banded wooden wheels, which made for an incredibly uncomfortable ride on the cobbled streets of the era. To increase speed, inventors enlarged the front wheel, leading to the iconic but precarious "penny-farthing" in the 1870s.

The true revolution occurred with the development of the "safety bicycle" in the late 1880s. Featuring two wheels of equal size and a chain-driven rear wheel, it offered stability and ease of use, making cycling accessible to a much wider demographic. The subsequent invention of pneumatic tires by John Boyd Dunlop further smoothed the ride, cementing the bicycle's place in modern society.
