## Overview

LuyenTap Academic Workspace is a calm, work-focused educational product. Its visual identity should feel like a serious academic desk: quiet, organized, scan-friendly, and respectful of long study sessions. It is not a playful learning toy, a marketing site, or a saturated SaaS dashboard.

The system has three coordinated modes:

- **Student workspace:** editorial, quiet, spacious, and text-led. The interface uses fog-gray page layers, white work surfaces, a precise violet accent, and thin separators.
- **Exam runtime:** utilitarian and paper-like. Reading passages use a serif text voice, question panels are compact, and controls are closer to browser form clarity than decorative app chrome.
- **Teacher authoring and grading:** denser and more operational. Builder screens use pale green-gray surfaces, teal primary actions, small status pills, and outlined panels that support repeated editing and review.

Older pastel and glass treatments may appear on legacy management surfaces, but the enduring identity is restrained academic utility. When in doubt, reduce ornament, increase legibility, and let content hierarchy carry the screen.

## Colors

The base palette is a cool academic neutral system. Page backgrounds are very light gray, shell surfaces are a slightly deeper fog gray, and primary work surfaces are white. Text uses blue-green charcoal rather than pure black for app chrome, while exam reading content may use true ink for maximum readability.

Violet is the main student accent. Use it sparingly for active navigation, selected tabs, focused highlights, and the most important student-facing action. Teal is the teacher authoring accent and should signal builder progress, publish readiness, and primary editor actions. Exam blue is reserved for test-taking controls, selected answers, and passage/question synchronization.

Status colors should be calm containers first and saturated text second. Use pale green for answered or ready states, pale amber for warnings, and pale red for blocking errors. Avoid flooding the interface with bright fills; most state should appear as chips, thin borders, or subtle surface washes.

## Typography

The primary UI typeface is Inter with system fallbacks. It should feel crisp and editorial, with strong titles, compact labels, and readable body copy. Avoid playful display typography.

Use heavy weights for page titles and key navigation labels, but keep body text regular and calm. Micro-labels are uppercase with letter spacing and should be used for sidebar labels, section eyebrows, status labels, tabs, and metadata. They should feel precise, not loud.

Reading passages are the exception: use a traditional serif such as Georgia or Times New Roman for long-form exam text. This creates a print-paper reading rhythm and visually separates passage content from the surrounding app controls.

## Layout & Spacing

The layout rhythm is based on a 4px grid with common steps at 8px, 12px, 16px, 24px, 32px, 48px, and 64px. Student pages should feel breathable, with generous side padding and clear section breaks. Teacher pages can be denser, but related controls must still be grouped inside obvious panels.

Student workspace layouts should preserve a three-part mental model: navigation, central work area, and contextual support area. The center is the main reading and action surface. The side areas are quieter and should not compete visually with the main task.

Exam screens prioritize space efficiency. Split-pane desktop layouts should keep passage and question regions distinct with plain dividers. Mobile exam screens should compress the same workflow through sticky headers, passage tabs, a prominent questions action, and a bottom sheet for answering.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth is mostly created through tonal layering and borders, not heavy shadows. Student surfaces are nearly flat: gray page, white content, whisper borders, and minimal shadow. This keeps the workspace composed and reduces visual noise during study.

Teacher builder panels may use soft elevation to distinguish editing regions from the page background. Menus, dialogs, review panels, and bottom sheets may use stronger shadows, but the shadow should remain diffused and practical.

Glassmorphism, decorative blur, shimmer, and floating-card effects are not part of the core product identity. If a legacy surface uses them, treat them as local historical styling rather than the direction for new work.

## Shapes

The default shape language is rectangular with modest softness. Standard controls use 6px to 8px radius. Panels and cards use 8px to 12px radius. Dialogs, mobile sheets, and large transient surfaces may use 16px to 24px radius.

Exam runtime controls are sharper, often 2px to 6px, to preserve a formal test-paper feel. Student shell controls may use pill shapes for badges, avatars, and primary mobile actions, but avoid making every control pill-shaped. Teacher builder buttons should stay compact and utilitarian.

## Components

### Buttons

Primary student buttons use violet, white text, compact height, and restrained radius. Teacher primary buttons use teal. Exam primary actions use deep ink or exam blue and should be visually decisive. Secondary buttons are usually white with a thin border and dark text.

### Cards and Panels

Cards are work surfaces, not decoration. Student cards should be almost flat, using white or tonal backgrounds and subtle separators. Teacher cards can be more framed because they organize form-heavy workflows. Avoid nested card stacks unless the nested surface has a clear editing or review purpose.

### Navigation

Navigation should be quiet and precise. Active states use a small accent edge, underline, or tonal fill rather than oversized colored blocks. Labels are compact and often uppercase. Icons should be simple line icons; avoid emoji navigation.

### Forms

Inputs use white backgrounds, thin outlines, and compact vertical rhythm. Labels are small, bold, and utilitarian. Focus states should be visible through border color and a soft halo, not through dramatic glow.

### Tabs and Segmented Controls

Student tabs should feel editorial: slim labels, underlines, and generous horizontal spacing. Teacher builder tabs may use segmented fills because they represent steps or editing modes. Exam passage tabs are full-width, plain, and highly legible.

### Exam Question Controls

Question options should look dependable and answer-sheet-like. Use white controls with thin borders, blue selected states, clear radio or checkbox affordances, and strong question numbers. Mobile chips must be large enough to tap confidently.

### Overlays and Bottom Sheets

Mobile bottom sheets should feel sturdy and purposeful. Use a dim backdrop, a white sheet, rounded top corners, a visible handle, sticky headers when needed, and strong fixed action rows for submission or review decisions.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do keep the product academic, composed, and information-first.
- Do use violet for student active states and teal for teacher authoring actions.
- Do keep exam pages plain, readable, and close to paper-based test expectations.
- Do use tonal layers, thin borders, and whitespace before adding shadows.
- Do preserve a minimum 44px touch target for mobile controls.
- Do make long-reading surfaces highly legible before adding decorative styling.
- Don't use gradient backgrounds as the default workspace surface.
- Don't use glass, blur, shimmer, or floating card effects for new core product screens.
- Don't turn student pages into KPI dashboards or social-feed clones.
- Don't mix multiple strong accent palettes in the same workflow.
- Don't over-round dense teacher forms or exam controls.
- Don't hide important workflow state inside color alone; pair state color with clear text.
